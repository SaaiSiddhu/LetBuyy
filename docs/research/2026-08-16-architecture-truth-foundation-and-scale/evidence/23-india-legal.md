## Summary
The single most consequential finding is that the DPDP Act 2023 is still overwhelmingly NOT in force as of 2026-08-16. MeitY's commencement notification G.S.R. 843(E) of 13 November 2025 brought into force only section 1(2), section 2 (definitions), sections 18-26 (the Data Protection Board), sections 35, 38-43 and section 44(1) and (3); every substantive obligation LetBuyy would engineer for - notice (s.5), consent (s.6), the general obligations of a Data Fiduciary including security and breach notification (s.8), children's data (s.9), Significant Data Fiduciary duties (s.10), data-principal rights (ss.11-14), transfer outside India (s.16) and the penalty regime (ss.28-34) - sits in the eighteen-month bucket and commences on or about 13-14 May 2027, with the Consent Manager provisions (s.6(9), Rule 4) at twelve months, on or about 13-14 November 2026. The DPDP Rules 2025 (G.S.R. 846(E)) mirror this exactly: only Rules 1, 2 and 17-21 are live, and those are Board-internal. Because DPDP s.44(2) is itself in the eighteen-month bucket, IT Act section 43A and the SPDI Rules 2011 have NOT been repealed and remain the operative personal-data security and grievance law binding LetBuyy today, alongside IT Act s.72A. What genuinely binds LetBuyy right now is therefore not DPDP but the CERT-In Directions of 28 April 2022 - a mandatory 6-hour cyber-incident report to CERT-In, NIC/NPL time synchronisation, a designated Point of Contact, and ICT logs enabled and maintained securely for a rolling 180 days within Indian jurisdiction. On the platform-versus-processor question, the statutory test is purely who "determines the purpose and means of processing" (s.2(i)) versus who processes "on behalf of a Data Fiduciary" (s.2(k)); it turns on decision rights, not on data custody, contract labels or fund flows, and s.2(i)'s "alone or in conjunction with other persons" means LetBuyy can be a joint Data Fiduciary while the merchant is one too - with s.8(1) making each responsible "irrespective of any agreement to the contrary" and no GDPR-style allocation mechanism available. In practice LetBuyy is likely a Processor for merchant-instructed order and customer data but a Data Fiduciary in its own right for its merchant account holders and for any cross-tenant analytics, cross-merchant fraud scoring, ML training, its own marketing or unilaterally-set retention - and that classification is what decides who owes erasure and breach notice, because the Act imposes no direct duty on a Processor at all. On payments, the RBI (Regulation of Payment Aggregators) Directions 2025 of 15 September 2025 define a PA by the conjunctive act of aggregating customer payments and "subsequently settl[ing] the collected funds to such merchants", and expressly carve out at paras 4.j and 10.g the payment gateway that "provides technology infrastructure to route and facilitate processing of a payment transaction without any involvement in handling of funds" - so a platform where the merchant is merchant-of-record on the merchant's own Razorpay account and LetBuyy never custodies funds sits outside the authorisation perimeter, with risk arising only if LetBuyy ever routes, holds, nets fees from, or splits merchant collections. The Directions also removed the fixed regulatory T+n settlement schedule, which is now purely contractual between PA and merchant. Two RBI rules reach LetBuyy indirectly: the card-on-file restriction (no entity in the card chain other than issuers and networks may store CoF data, since 1 October 2022) and the 2018 payment-data localisation circular, whose FAQ extends the "stored only in India" obligation through PSOs to their service providers, intermediaries, payment gateways and third-party vendors. On GST, the decisive TCS test in s.52(1) is cumulative and fails on its last limb for LetBuyy: TCS bites only "where the consideration with respect to such supplies is to be collected by the said operator", so a SaaS platform that never collects consideration owes no TCS, needs no s.24(x) registration, and files no GSTR-8 or GSTR-9B - though whether it is an "electronic commerce operator" at all under the broader s.2(45) remains arguable and unlitigated for storefront SaaS, and s.9(5) reaches only notified services, never goods. E-invoicing is far narrower than commonly assumed: the Rule 48(4) threshold remains AATO above Rs 5 crore since 1 August 2023, it applies only to B2B, export, deemed-export and SEZ supplies and never to B2C, so most D2C storefront sales sit outside it entirely, while the 30-day IRN reporting cut-off applies only at AATO of Rs 10 crore and above from 1 April 2025, and records must be retained 72 months from the annual-return due date under s.36. For PCI DSS, v4.0.1 is the only active version with all future-dated requirements effective since 31 March 2025, and both redirect and TPSP-iframe checkouts can keep merchants on SAQ A - the script-safety eligibility criterion applies only to the iframe case and can be discharged by a Razorpay attestation - but LetBuyy's theme and app system permitting arbitrary scripts on the checkout page is the concrete risk that would push every merchant to SAQ A-EP. For messaging, TCCCPR 2018 as amended on 12 February 2025 now confines "transactional" to messages within thirty minutes of a customer-initiated transaction, treats shipping and delivery updates as "service" messages, and reclassifies any transactional or service message carrying promotional content as wholly promotional - so appending a coupon to a delivery SMS is a direct compliance failure - while unregistered senders are deemed to be sending UCC per se. Finally on residency: there is no general Indian mandate requiring commerce or personal data to be stored in India, DPDP's transfer provisions are a not-yet-commenced blacklist mechanism with no country notified, and Rule 13(4)'s localisation applies only to Significant Data Fiduciaries for categories the Government has not yet specified, so a single ap-south region is an architectural and contractual choice for commerce data - but it IS a legal requirement for ICT logs under CERT-In and for payment system data under the RBI 2018 circular flowing down through Razorpay. Every legal conclusion in this brief is source material only and is marked LEGAL-REVIEW-REQUIRED.

### [DPDP] DECISIVE QUESTION - Data Fiduciary vs Data Processor. s.2(i): 'Data Fiduciary means any person who alone or in conjunction with other persons determines the purpose and means of processing of personal data.' s.2(k): 'Data Processor means any person who processes personal data on behalf of a Data Fiduciary.' The test is WHO DETERMINES PURPOSE AND MEANS - not who holds the database, not the contract label, not who custodies funds. Consequence: the Act imposes NO direct statutory duty on a Processor. Breach notice (s.8(6)/Rule 7), erasure (s.8(7)), rights response (ss.11-14), grievance redressal (s.8(10)) and DPO publication (Rule 9) are all duties OF THE DATA FIDUCIARY; s.8(7)(b) requires the Fiduciary to 'cause its Data Processor to erase'; s.8(2) requires a valid contract before engaging a processor; Rule 6(1)(f) requires safeguards flow-down in that contract.
- Applies to LetBuyy: **DEPENDS - and it splits by data set, not by company. (1) For a merchant's customer order/address/phone data used solely to run that merchant's store, LetBuyy is most likely a Data Processor and the merchant the Data Fiduciary. (2) LetBuyy is a Data Fiduciary IN ITS OWN RIGHT (or jointly - s.2(i) expressly says 'alone or in conjunction with other persons') for any processing whose purpose IT sets: cross-tenant analytics/benchmarking, cross-merchant fraud/risk scoring, ML training, its own product telemetry, its own marketing to shoppers, unilaterally-set retention schedules, or a single shopper identity shared across storefronts. (3) LetBuyy is unambiguously a Data Fiduciary for its own merchant-account holders' personal data.**
- In force as of 2026-08-16: **s.2 definitions ARE in force now (from 14 Nov 2025). The consequences that flow from the classification (ss.8-17) are not in force until ~13-14 May 2027.**
- Flag: LEGAL-REVIEW-REQUIRED
- Source: https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf
- Note: What it turns on, concretely: (a) does LetBuyy decide the PURPOSE for anything beyond executing merchant instructions; (b) does it decide the MEANS in more than a technical-implementation sense (retention periods, what is collected, who else sees it, secondary use); (c) does it use tenant data for its own account. s.8(1) makes a Data Fiduciary responsible 'irrespective of any agreement to the contrary' - merchants cannot push their responsibility onto LetBuyy, and LetBuyy cannot buy processor status by calling itself one in the MSA. There is NO GDPR Art.26-style joint-controller allocation mechanism. CAUTION: Illustration (I) to s.8(7) treats Y, operator of an online marketplace ('an e-commerce service provider'), as the Data Fiduciary for a customer who registered on it. This classification is precisely what decides who owes erasure and breach notice: a Processor's exposure is contractual/indemnity only, a Fiduciary's is statutory and penalty-bearing.

### [DPDP] Breach notification (s.8(6), Rule 7). To each affected Data Principal: 'without delay', concise/clear/plain, via her user account or a registered communication channel, containing (a) description incl. nature, extent and timing of occurrence, (b) the consequences relevant to her, (c) mitigation measures implemented and being implemented, (d) safety measures she may take, (e) business contact information of a person who can respond to her queries. To the Board: (a) WITHOUT DELAY, a description incl. nature, extent, timing, LOCATION of occurrence and likely impact; and (b) WITHIN SEVENTY-TWO HOURS of becoming aware (extendable only on a written request to the Board): updated and detailed description, broad facts of the events/circumstances/reasons, mitigation measures implemented or proposed, any findings on who caused the breach, remedial measures to prevent recurrence, and a report on the intimations given to affected Data Principals.

### [DPDP] Significant Data Fiduciary (s.10, Rule 13). ### [RBI-PA] DECISIVE QUESTION - what triggers PA authorisation. RBI (Regulation of Payment Aggregators) Directions, 2025 (RBI/DPSS/2025-26/141, CO.DPSS.POLC.No.S-633/02-14-008/2025-26, dated 15 Sept 2025), para 4.i defines a Payment Aggregator as an entity that 'facilitates aggregation of payments made by customers to merchants through one or more payment channels through the merchant's interface (physical / virtual) for purchase of goods, services or investment products, AND SUBSEQUENTLY SETTLES THE COLLECTED FUNDS TO SUCH MERCHANTS.' Para 3.a: 'These Directions shall apply to all bank and non-bank entities undertaking the business of Payment Aggregator (PA) as defined herein.' Para 5: non-bank entities must obtain RBI authorisation before commencing PA business.
- Applies to LetBuyy: **NO on the facts described - LetBuyy appears OUTSIDE the PA licensing perimeter. The definition is conjunctive and turns on COLLECTING AND SETTLING MERCHANT FUNDS. Where the merchant is merchant-of-record on the merchant's own authorised PA (Razorpay) account, funds move customer -> Razorpay escrow -> merchant, and LetBuyy never receives, holds, nets against or settles those funds, LetBuyy does not undertake 'the business of PA'.**
- In force as of 2026-08-16: **IN FORCE. Effective 15 September 2025; supersedes the 2020/2021 PA-PG guidelines and the 2023 cross-border PA directions.**
- Flag: LEGAL-REVIEW-REQUIRED
- Source: https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12896
- Note: Operating PA business without authorisation contravenes the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007. The facts that would DRAG LetBuyy inside the perimeter: routing settlement through any LetBuyy-controlled bank account; holding merchant balances even transiently; deducting platform fees out of merchant collections BEFORE settlement; LetBuyy-directed split settlement; store credit or wallet balances redeemable for money; or LetBuyy becoming merchant-of-record. The 0% transaction fee is helpful evidence of the model but is not itself the legal test - fund custody is.

Circular 'Storage of Payment System Data', RBI/2017-18/153, DPSS.CO.OD No.2785/06.08.005/2017-2018, dated 6 April 2018: all authorised payment system providers shall ensure that THE ENTIRE DATA RELATING TO PAYMENT SYSTEMS OPERATED BY THEM IS STORED IN A SYSTEM ONLY IN INDIA. Per the RBI FAQ the scope reaches banks as payment system operators or participants plus 'system participants, service providers, intermediaries, payment gateways, third party vendors' engaged by authorised entities; 'entire data' covers end-to-end transaction details, customer information (name, contact details, identity numbers), payment-sensitive data (customer and beneficiary account details), credentials such as OTPs and passwords, and transaction metadata such as timestamps and amounts. Processing abroad is permitted but the resulting data shall be stored only in India after processing, and must be DELETED FROM THE SYSTEMS ABROAD AND BROUGHT BACK TO INDIA not later than one business day or 24 hours from payment processing, whichever is earlier. For cross-border transactions a copy of the domestic component may also be stored abroad if required. The PSO bears responsibility for compliance by the intermediaries and vendors it engages. The PA Directions 2025 expressly require PAs to comply with this circular.
- Applies to LetBuyy: **DEPENDS - not directly (LetBuyy is not an authorised PSO), but DERIVATIVELY and contractually: if LetBuyy sits in Razorpay's payment chain as a technology vendor or gateway, Razorpay is obliged to ensure LetBuyy's compliance for any payment system data LetBuyy touches or stores.**
- In force as of 2026-08-16: **IN FORCE since 2018 and actively enforced - RBI has imposed and subsequently lifted supervisory business restrictions citing this circular.**
- Flag: LEGAL-REVIEW-REQUIRED
- Source: https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/english/scripts/FAQs.aspx?Id=2995
- Note: This is SPECIFIC to payment system data - it does not reach product catalogues, storefront content, themes or CMS assets. The hard boundary question for LetBuyy is an ORDER RECORD carrying both commerce fields and payment references/customer identifiers: legal review is needed on whether such a record is 'data relating to payment systems'. Practically, keeping order and payment tables in ap-south removes the question. Circular text: https://rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=11244

### [residency] EXCEPTION 2 - CERT-In Directions, 28 April 2022 (No. 20(3)/2022-CERT-In, under IT Act s.70B(6)), binding on 'service providers, intermediaries, data centres, body corporate and Government organisations'. Direction (i): connect to the NTP server of NIC or NPL (or traceable sources that do not deviate) to synchronise all ICT system clocks. Direction (ii): 'shall mandatorily report cyber incidents as mentioned in Annexure I to CERT-In WITHIN 6 HOURS of noticing such incidents or being brought to notice about such incidents.' Direction (iii): designate a Point of Contact to interface with CERT-In in the Annexure II format, keep it updated, and comply with CERT-In orders including near-real-time information requests. Direction (iv): 'shall mandatorily enable logs of all their ICT systems and maintain them securely FOR A ROLLING PERIOD OF 180 DAYS and the same SHALL BE MAINTAINED WITHIN THE INDIAN JURISDICTION', to be provided to CERT-In with any incident report or on direction. Direction (v): data centres, Virtual Private Server providers, cloud service providers and VPN service providers must register and retain accurate subscriber/customer information for five years (or longer if mandated) after cancellation or withdrawal of registration.
- Applies to LetBuyy: **YES, DIRECTLY AND TODAY. LetBuyy is both a 'body corporate' and a 'service provider'. This - not DPDP - is the binding incident-reporting clock (6 hours) and the binding log-residency mandate (180 days within Indian jurisdiction) in August 2026.**
- In force as of 2026-08-16: **IN FORCE since 28 June 2022 (60 days after issue), and from 25 September 2022 for MSMEs and for the subscriber-validation mechanism.**
- Flag: LEGAL-REVIEW-REQUIRED
- Source: https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/CERT-In_Directions_70B_28.04.2022.pdf
- Note: THIS IS THE ANSWER TO 'is ap-south a legal requirement': for ICT LOGS, yes - they must be maintained within Indian jurisdiction for a rolling 180 days, so shipping logs to a non-Indian observability SaaS is a live compliance issue. The 6-hour CERT-In clock is far tighter than DPDP's future 72-hour Board report, so incident runbooks must be built to 6 hours now. The PA Directions additionally require annual cyber-security audit by a CERT-In empanelled auditor and monthly incident reporting for PAs, which flows down contractually to LetBuyy.
