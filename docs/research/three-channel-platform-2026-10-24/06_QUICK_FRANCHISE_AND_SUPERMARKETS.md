# Quick franchise and supermarket operating models

## Preserve the earlier supermarket concept

The earlier [Agrimore concept synthesis](/Volumes/Projects/LetBuyy/docs/evidence/audits/2026-09-19-unified-commerce/AGRIMORE_UNIFIED_COMMERCE_CONCEPT_SYNTHESIS_2026-09-19.md:151) already describes supermarkets contributing premises, stock, procurement and staff while the platform supplies digital demand, stock/order coordination, delivery and support. It also distinguishes stock ownership from custody. This research develops that model; it does not replace it with an assumed company-owned dark-store chain.

Its illustrative ₹600 basket and claimed contribution were explicitly unvalidated. They are not imported as market benchmarks. The present model uses separately labelled sensitivity inputs and includes costs that an existing supermarket can otherwise overlook, including extra picking capacity, shrink, support, failed trips and cash delays.

## Four models that need different contracts

| Model | Merchandise owner and seller | Premises/staff operator | Money direction | Initial fit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Partner supermarket | Existing independent supermarket, unless otherwise contracted | Supermarket with assigned Quick capacity | Seller receives goods proceeds; pays agreed software/demand/fulfillment services | Recommended lower-investment pilot |
| Franchisee-owned dark store | Independent franchise business, matching current owner direction | Franchisee | Franchisee funds stock/operations and pays defined brand/platform services | Plan after local economics are demonstrated |
| Managed operator | Approved merchandise seller; operator may own no goods | Contracted operator | Operator can receive operating compensation; other service flows separately defined | Optional, not the default inventory-owning model |
| Multi-owner custody hub | Multiple merchants retain ownership under explicit agreements | Partner node/custodian | Separate goods proceeds, custody and fulfillment obligations | Later extension; not admitted by a simple location row |

A landlord leasing a building is not a fifth merchandise seller. A supplier selling wholesale goods is not automatically the franchise operator. The customer invoice must identify the actual seller and applicable responsible entities even when LetKart is the visible consumer brand.

## Responsibility matrix to close before activation

| Responsibility | Must identify | Required evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Premises | Leaseholder, permits, utilities, access and safety | Actual usable site, agreement and opening checks |
| Setup | Shelving, cold equipment, scanners, networking and backup power | Itemized vendor quotes, ownership and maintenance |
| Merchandise | Buyer of stock, supplier terms, working capital and title | Purchase/receipt and owner-scoped stock records |
| Operations | Staffing, shifts, pick/pack quality and supervision | Trained people and rehearsed procedures |
| Delivery | Rider contracting, cost, waiting, failed attempts and returns | Accepted dispatch/custody and cost policy |
| Loss | Expiry, spoilage, theft, shortage, damage and insurance | Attributable incident and adjustment rules |
| Customer remedies | Cancellation, replacement if later supported, refunds and complaints | Real support and refund authority |
| Payments | Provider contract, beneficiary, COD collector and remittance | Approved flow and reconciliation proof |
| Brand | Licensed rights, service standards and permitted marketing | Cleared brand and signed applicable agreement |
| Exit | Stock withdrawal, customer obligations, equipment and security | Termination/recovery procedure without lost obligations |

## Partner supermarket pilot

Start with a selected store, exact service hours, a bounded approved SKU list and measured packing capacity. Map the existing catalog into canonical variants and locations. Reconcile an opening physical count and integrate counter sales before exposing a shared pool. Where reliable till integration is unavailable, use a physically protected Quick allocation with clear replenishment; a stale spreadsheet is not shared inventory.

Designate a picking area, package staging and chilled handling. The partner assigns accountable staff rather than asking an occupied cashier to notice app notifications between counter customers. Use the dedicated Ops app for receiving, queue, picking, packing, exceptions, handover and returns. Delivery remains the existing separate rider app connected to canonical assignment and custody.

Measure incremental expenses and fully allocated expenses separately. A supermarket may need only incremental space and one additional picker initially; its existing rent still matters when comparing long-term alternatives. Do not charge rent twice or count fixed picker salaries again as per-order picking labor in the same model.

## Dedicated franchise store

Admit a franchise site only after a reviewed catchment/demand hypothesis, premises/cold-chain suitability, itemized investment and cash runway. The franchisee's setup investment includes actual equipment and working capital, not only a brand fee. A one-time brand association charge requires real brand rights and delivered setup/training value; a promised future brand is not automatically worth a large fee.

The platform should publish a clear fee schedule and cost responsibility sheet before recruitment. Avoid guaranteed ROI, demand or exclusive territory claims unsupported by executed commitments. Node coverage priority is a serviceability configuration, not a property right or franchise territory guarantee.

## Operating lifecycle

Application → business/site verification → commercial selection → signed responsibility matrix → provider/beneficiary readiness → catalog and opening count → staff/client enrollment → coverage/hours configuration → rehearsal → controlled live intake → daily stock/cash close → measured expansion or correction.

Closing or pausing a node must preserve accepted orders, goods custody, customer support, refunds, cash close and statements. Partner exit needs actual stock return/withdrawal and unresolved-obligation reconciliation. Removing a user or hiding a storefront cannot perform that work.

## Multi-owner custody example

A clothing merchant sends 20 shirts to a supermarket fulfillment node. The merchant remains owner unless a real ownership transfer occurs. Node staff receive actual quantities, record shortage/damage and hold the goods under an explicit agreement. Quick consumes only that owner's accepted saleable stock. The supermarket does not gain access to the merchant's bank accounts or full customer database.

Allowing the node's counter to sell those shirts is an additional permission and commercial arrangement. Custody alone does not authorize counter selling. The first pilot should avoid this extension until the simpler supermarket-owned stock model is reconciled and accepted.

## Commercial recommendation

Use a supermarket partnership first to observe demand and service costs. Offer a modest transparent software agreement plus one clearly priced variable service where applicable. Build a dedicated-franchise offer only after actual data supports an attractive operator outcome. Managed-operator compensation remains a separate model: the existence of a competitor partner programme does not tell LetBuyy whether it should pay or charge a particular counterparty.
