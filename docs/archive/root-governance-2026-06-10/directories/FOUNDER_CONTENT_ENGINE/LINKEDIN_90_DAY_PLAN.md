### Post 3 (Fri) — Why start in college

**HOOK:** Everyone said: finish college first, then build. I think that's backwards.

**POST:**

No mortgage. No dependents. A built-in excuse to talk to anyone ("I'm a student researching X"). And the highest tolerance for failure I'll ever have, because the downside is just… a lesson.

**LESSON:** The best time to take an asymmetric risk is when your downside is smallest. For most people, that's *now*, not later.

### Post 5 (Wed) — What "foundations" actually means
**Bucket:** Building In Public · **Canva:** Architecture Breakdown ("Inside LetBuyy")

**HOOK:** "Just launch already." I hear it a lot. Here's what "foundations" means and why I won't skip them.

**POST:**
When people say "just launch," they're picturing the visible 10% — the storefront, the buy button. They're not picturing the 90% that decides whether the visible part survives contact with real money.

Foundations, in my world, means:
→ **Identity** — knowing exactly who a user is and what they're allowed to do. Get this wrong and you don't have bugs, you have breaches.
→ **Checkout integrity** — money movement that's correct *every* time, not most of the time.
→ **A design system** — so every screen feels like one product, not five.

I've seen what happens when startups skip these to hit a launch date: they spend the next year firefighting the exact things they rushed.

I'd rather be slow now than on fire later. Especially when the product touches a merchant's money.

**LESSON:** Speed is a strategy for the visible layer. For the layer that touches money and identity, *correctness* is the strategy.

**CTA:** Where's the line for you — when is "ship fast" right, and when is it reckless?

**Tanglish accent:** *Money and identity la "fast" venaam. "Correct" venum.*

## WEEK 3 — The Why (Merchant Problem)

### Post 7 (Mon) — The merchant problem, named
**Bucket:** Merchant Education · **Canva:** Architecture Breakdown ("Inside LetBuyy")

**HOOK:** An Indian merchant loses money in 4 invisible ways. Most tools fix zero of them.

**POST:**
When a small merchant sells online in India, the money leaks happen where they can't see:

1. **Settlement delay** — the sale is done, but the cash lands days later. Cash flow dies in that gap.
2. **Hidden fees** — payment cuts, platform charges, and convenience fees stacked until margin quietly vanishes.
3. **Logistics chaos** — RTO (return-to-origin) on COD orders silently eats profit.
4. **No single source of truth** — orders here, payments there, inventory in a notebook.

None of these show up as a scary number on a dashboard. They show up as a merchant who works harder every month and somehow keeps less.

LetBuyy's reason to exist is to make these four leaks *visible and smaller*. I'm still building toward it. But this is the north star.

**LESSON:** The most dangerous business problems aren't the loud ones. They're the quiet, recurring leaks nobody put on a dashboard.

**CTA:** Merchants — which of these four hurts you most? I'll go deep on whichever gets the most replies.

**Tanglish accent:** *Naalu invisible leak. Merchant ku theriyaadhu, but margin pogudhu.*

**Visual:** 4-panel carousel-style single image, one leak per quadrant, violet/amber. Canva: Inside LetBuyy template.

### Post 8 (Wed) — UPI & COD reality
**Bucket:** Merchant Education · **Canva:** Startup Lesson ("What I Learned Today")

**HOOK:** UPI changed Indian commerce forever. But it didn't fix the thing merchants worry about most.

**POST:**
UPI is a miracle — instant, near-free, everywhere. It solved *acceptance*. A street vendor and a mall store now take payments the same way.

But acceptance was never the merchant's biggest fear. Their biggest fear is COD.

Cash on Delivery still dominates huge parts of Indian e-commerce because trust is built differently here — people want to see the product before they pay. And COD brings its own monster: returns. An order that ships, gets refused, and comes back has cost the merchant packing, shipping, *and* return shipping — for zero revenue.

So the real India commerce problem isn't "how do we accept payment." UPI mostly nailed that. It's "how do we handle the messy middle — COD, trust, returns — without bleeding the merchant."

That messy middle is exactly where I'm focusing.

**LESSON:** When a famous solution (UPI) solves one layer, the real opportunity moves to the *next* layer nobody's talking about yet.

**CTA:** Do you still choose COD when you shop online? Curious how this is changing.

**Tanglish accent:** *UPI payment solve panniduchu. But COD-um returns-um? Adhu dhaan real game.*

**Visual:** "UPI solved acceptance. The next problem is trust + returns." Two-stage diagram. Canva: Startup Lesson template.

### Post 9 (Fri) — Why fees matter so much
**Bucket:** Merchant Education · **Canva:** Founder Update

**HOOK:** A 2% fee sounds small. To a merchant on thin margins, it can be the entire profit.

**POST:**
Here's math nobody shows the merchant up front.

Say a merchant makes 8% net margin (common for small retail). A platform takes a 2% cut on the sale. That 2% isn't 2% of their *profit* — it's a quarter of it. Gone. Before logistics, before returns, before the time they spent.

Now stack a payment fee, a "convenience" fee, maybe a listing fee. Suddenly the merchant is working a full day to hand most of the upside to platforms.

This is why I'm almost obsessive about fee transparency in how I'm designing LetBuyy. Not because low fees are a marketing line — but because for a small Indian merchant, the fee structure *is* the business model. Get it wrong and you're not a tool for them, you're another leak.

I haven't earned the right to claim victory here. But I've earned the right to care about it loudly.

**LESSON:** A fee is never "just a percentage of the sale." It's a percentage of the merchant's *survival*.

**CTA:** What's a fee you've paid that quietly added up to way more than you expected?

**Tanglish accent:** *2% chinna number madhiri thonum. But margin la adhu kaal vaasi.*

**Visual:** Margin bar chart — "8% margin, then watch the fees eat it." Canva: Founder Update template, data variant.

## WORKED EXAMPLE — Week 5, Post 1 (so you see the target quality)
**Theme:** Architecture Decisions · **Canva:** Inside LetBuyy

**HOOK:** This week I made an architecture decision I'll either thank or curse myself for in a year.

**POST:**
Every system has a moment where you choose between "fast now" and "flexible later." I hit mine this week.

The question: how tightly should checkout, payments, and orders be coupled? Tight coupling is faster to build and easier to reason about today. Loose coupling is more work now but survives change — and in Indian payments, *change is the only constant* (new rails, new COD rules, new settlement flows).

I chose the harder path: clear boundaries between money-movement, cart, and order state. It cost me three extra days this week and a lot of self-doubt.

Ask me in a year if it was worth it. But my bet is that the thing touching a merchant's money should be the *least* tangled part of the system.

**LESSON:** "Fast now vs. flexible later" is the most repeated decision in building. For the parts touching money or identity, bias toward *later*.

**CTA:** Engineers — where do you draw the coupling line? I'm genuinely still calibrating.

**Tanglish accent:** *Fast now vs flexible later — money part la naan "later" choose pannen.*
