## 1. Executive summary

**The LetBuyy governance is excellent law that is not in force.** The seven-document rewrite (`GOVERNANCE_REWRITE_2026_06_10/`) is, as *written law*, strong: its authority chain points the right direction in all 8 docs, every canonical winner is named consistently on *system identity* (33/33 domains), the legacy-elimination layer is the most complete artifact in the corpus (77/78 rows fully dispositioned with gates and rollbacks), and the roadmap's critical spine is correctly ordered. The phase audits found **zero architecture errors** — every one of the 129 findings is a *governance-mechanics* defect (a stale clause, a duplicated fact with no canonical owner, a missing decision-home, an unbacked rule), and every prescribed fix is a surgical edit, a validator, or a forced decision. **Nothing here re-litigates a winner.**

**But the governance an agent actually inhabits today is the *rejected* architecture, and the machine that gates it is adversarial to the new law.** This is the single load-bearing fact of the whole program, established by Phase 6 (CODEX) and Phase 7 (VAL):

- `CLAUDE.md` → "follow `AGENTS.md` first." The live root `AGENTS.md` is the 2026-06-08 **scan-generated** file that declares the 9-app topology canonical, the CQRS cluster governed, and CQRS manifest-ownership *required*. **AGENTS_V2 is referenced by no file an agent traverses — discoverability of the new law ≈ 0%.**
- `scripts/validators/governance-common.mjs` (imported by all ~187 validators) hardcodes the rejected 9 apps + 6 reserved services + the CQRS package cluster as "canonical." The shared truth-source *is* the drift.
- `runtime-manifest-validator.mjs` **fails CI unless** every manifest delegates to `application-services`/`runtime-composition`. The gate doesn't tolerate CQRS — it **requires** it.
- `repository-intelligence-validator.mjs` **hard-asserts the drift strings are present** in `AGENTS.md`. **So the act of promoting AGENTS_V2 — deleting those strings — fails CI.** The governance is mechanically locked to its own falsehood.
- ~70 of the ~187 validators (≈37–47%) are **theater**: they certify replay/projection/saga/CRDT/failover/WAL/multi-region machinery the Constitution forbids. Of the 23 Constitutional Rules, **~2 are fully enforced (~10%)**; every money rule, the identity sole-issuer rule, and the provider-honesty rule have **ZERO** enforcement.

**The one self-blocking gate.** "Codex safety" is gated entirely on **GATE-1** (promote AGENTS_V2 → root `AGENTS.md`, retarget the validators, retire the scan generator), and **GATE-1 is currently blocked by the very validators it must retarget** (`repository-intelligence-validator` asserts the strings that promotion deletes). GATE-1 therefore **cannot be executed by editing `AGENTS.md` alone**; it must be one atomic commit that swaps the file *and* retargets the three drift-locking validators *and* retires the generator together — or CI makes the promotion impossible. This is **HW-0**, the highest-leverage action in the program: until it lands, the quality of the seven docs is irrelevant to what an agent can be stopped from doing.

**The path (four waves):**
- **HW-0 — the GATE-1 atomic change-set.** One commit: promote AGENTS_V2, retarget the 3 drift-locking validators, retire the scan generator, delete the ~70 theater validators paired with their dead-package deletion, fix the deploy matrix. This flips the machine from enforcing the wrong architecture to permitting the right one. *Blocked-by-ratification (it IS the ratification act).*
- **HW-1 — enforce + authority + the forced ops-console decision.** The money/security validators that **ship before promotion** (they don't depend on it and are the cheapest large risk cut), plus the authority/RATIFIED-status fixes, plus GATE-OPS-SURVIVOR forcing the one undetermined fate.
- **HW-2 — consistency + legacy completeness + roadmap reorder.** The single-source-of-truth workstream (Constitution owns each fact once; others cite), the ~5 new LEG rows that close the coverage seam, and the roadmap dependency/wave reconciliations.
- **HW-3 — full validator buildout + de-dup polish.** The remaining new canonical validators, the retarget/delete completion, and the final consistency polish.

**Documents-vs-enforcement framing (§7):** the seven docs are the *intended* law; the live `AGENTS.md` + validator suite are the *in-force* law, and today they disagree completely. Categories A/B/C (authority, consistency, amendments) harden the *documents*; category D (validators) and category E (forced decisions) harden the *enforcement*. **A perfect document layer changes nothing an agent can be stopped by until category D lands.** Governance is "hardened" only when the in-force law equals the intended law — defined precisely in §8.

**Consolidation result: 129 findings → 54 actions** (A: 11 · B: 9 · C: 13 · D: 17 · E: 4). The 129→54 compression comes from merging the ~6 cross-report overlaps the directive named (ops-console, identity-path, GATE-1 self-block, single-source-of-truth, RATIFIED-status) plus the heavy overlap between the CODEX "what's unenforced" findings and the VAL "what validator closes it" findings (these are the *same* gaps seen from two angles — CODEX-G-005 ↔ five VAL money findings, CODEX-G-006 ↔ VAL identity findings, etc. — and collapse into one validator action each).

## 7. Documents-vs-enforcement framing

The seven-document rewrite and the live repository are **two different bodies of law**, and the whole program is the work of making them equal:

- **The intended law** = `GOVERNANCE_REWRITE_2026_06_10/` (Constitution → AGENTS_V2 → Canonical Systems → Legacy+Roadmap → UI/UX → evidence). It is hand-authored, internally near-consistent, and chooses the right architecture. **It is what the docs *say*.**
- **The in-force law** = the live root `AGENTS.md` (scan-generated, 9-app, CQRS-required) + the ~187-validator suite (47% theater, inverted on topology/deploy/domain) + `production-deploy.yml` (9-app matrix) + `CLAUDE.md` (routes to the generated file). **It is what the machine *requires*.** Today it is the rejected architecture.

The five fix-categories map cleanly onto this split:

| Category | Hardens | Effect if done alone |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Authority** | the intended law's *self-consistency* (chain, masthead, DOC 07) | The docs agree with each other. **Changes nothing an agent can be stopped by.** |
| **B — Consistency** | the intended law's *single-source-of-truth* | A future edit can't silently fork a fact. **Changes nothing an agent can be stopped by.** |
| **C — Amendments** | the intended law's *completeness* (LEG rows, roadmap gates) | Every loser is dispositioned; the roadmap is sound. **Changes nothing an agent can be stopped by.** |
| **D — Validators** | the **in-force law** | **This is the only category that changes what an agent can be stopped from doing.** |
| **E — Forced decisions** | the seam where the intended law is *deliberately incomplete* (UNVERIFIED) | Removes the one class of "no fate / no value-set" that no edit can resolve. |

**The load-bearing consequence:** categories A, B, C are necessary (a self-contradictory or incomplete law cannot be safely enforced) but **insufficient** — they perfect a document no agent reads. **Only category D, gated entirely on HW-0, flips the in-force law.** This is why HW-0 is the whole game and why the money validators (HARD-042/044) are singled out as `shippable-now`: they are the only category-D work that does not wait on promotion, and they close the highest catastrophe-to-effort gap in the repository (a money path can today post nowhere, charge a 3% rake, or custody funds with a fully green gate).
